On Tuesday, I finished *the Hobbit*.

## Spoilers:
Though it is clearly a children's book, it is very well written; the narrative voice was excellent.
The story was fun enough, with the middle bit being my favourite (The Goblins, the Ring and the Escape from Mirkwood), though the beginning was also noteworthy. The end was not quite my cup of tea, as Bard was very conveniently introduced when the dragon needed slaying, and then slays the dragon while Thorin's group are just hiding elsewhere.

As Laketown is destroyed, the survivors then (understandably) go and demand reparations from the dwarves, aided by the Elves, who just so happen to be marching an army to the Lonely Mountain for reasons which, though they may not have been spelled out clearly in the book (or I may have missed that), probably have to do with the hoard of treasure now only guarded by 13 measly small people, who also happened to have escaped the elves custody earlier.

The elves then unite with the men and they go and ask for a share of the bounty, as if the elves also deserve that.
The dwarves do not want to negotiate with the elves (understandably), so negotiations break down, with the dwarves blamed for it. The dwarves also summon an army, and the whole situation seems unsalvageable.

Cue convenient goblin/orc army which solves this by uniting all the armies and conveniently killing of Thorin and his line (as Thorin was the biggest obstacle in negotiating with the elves). The elves, wonderous they, are thanked, and get a reward. Bilbo leaves with Gandalf, and though invited, they skip Mirkwood, because the forest is so dark (the elves, of course, are wonderful). Instead, they go to Rivendell, where the book describes for one last time how wonderful the elves are, before Bilbo gets home.

At work, I have finished my tour of Frama-C, and I am now doing a few exercises which are relevant to the current research.
This led me to find out the hard way that, as far as I can see, the WP plugin (for deductive verification) seems to not support memory allocation. 
Finding this out ate most of my Friday, except the two hours spent on looking for my Rabobank card. Welp.

Anyways, now I am done with that tutorial, I will have to investigate (beyond the small investigation I have already done) how LLMs are made, for a project. I also still want to do the [Software Foundations series by Pierce et al.](https://softwarefoundations.cis.upenn.edu/), so there is enough to do.

Sadly, I get somewhat easily mentally tired these days, so my days at the office have not been the longest. 
I think this is because I am suffering from mild impostor syndrome; I feel quite a lot of pressure to contribute something meaningful to the group, to the extent that I keep trying to hurry myself, which is the last thing I should be doing.
Part of it can probably be solved by getting more comfortable in the office, and a part will just have to be forced through.

For the first part, soon, we will be moving to another office anyways, so let's see what kind of office that is.
I may have to get myself noise-cancelling headphones, however, and see if I cannot find a better spot than I have now.
For the second part, I think I'll just have to do my best.
In the Netherlands, when the pressure got a bit much, I used to go home and work a bit extra in some evening later in the week or in the weekend, but my room here (and its tiny desk) really does not facilitate this.
Let's start with allowing myself to work on the Software Foundations series at least two hours per day.
Maybe this normalizes me being at the office longer.
